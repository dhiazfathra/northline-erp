## SLOs

Targets a tier's benchmark must meet (ADR-0048). "Write" means posting a sales invoice, including projection catch-up.
"Report" means Neraca, Laba/Rugi and aging at the tier's volume. Workers and Neon publish no SLA percentage on their
price pages, so these are our targets, not vendor promises.

| Tier | Availability | RPO | RTO | Write p95 | Report p95 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T0 | best effort | seed is reproducible | — | — | — |
| T1 | 99.5% | ≤ 1 min inside Neon's 7-day restore; 24 h from R2 dumps | 4 h | 800 ms | 3 s |
| T2 | 99.9% | ≤ 1 min (30-day restore) | 1 h | 500 ms | 3 s |
| T3 | 99.9% | ≤ 1 min | 1 h | 500 ms | 5 s |
| T4 | 99.95% | ≤ 1 min (DR standby) | 15 min | 500 ms | 5 s |

## What changes in the runtime

- **T1.**
  - Tenant registry and hostname routing (N6).
  - Workers Paid lifts the CPU cap from 10 ms to 30 s, which closes risk R5 (PBKDF2 login).
  - Nightly R2 dumps (N2).
- **T2.**
  - Hyperdrive in front of Neon, which removes the per-request TCP and TLS connect (risk R3).
  - Neon Scale, autoscaling from 0.5 to 2 CU, with a 0.5 CU floor so there is no cold start in office hours.
- **T3.**
  - A Queues consumer runs projection `catchUp`, replacing the inline loop in `apps/api/src/worker.ts`.
  - `/api/reports/*` reads from a replica.
  - The events table is partitioned by month.
  - SSO and MFA (E2).
- **T4.**
  - Multi-company with consolidation (X8).
  - A DR standby in a second region through logical replication.
  - Closed periods archived out of the hot tables.

## What the numbers say

- **Infra stays under Rp 100k per user per month at every tier**, from Rp 34k at T1 to Rp 100k at T4.
- **On-premise costs about 2 times more at T3 and T4, and up to 8 times more at T1**, once ops staff is counted. Sell
  it for residency or policy reasons, priced as a project: the customer buys the hardware and pays for setup.
- **Don't compete with Accurate on price at the small end.** At 10 users, Northline's cost to serve (Rp 940k with ops)
  is above Accurate's list price (Rp 533k). T0 and T1 are the funnel: trial and Parallel Close.
- **Price T2 from Rp 5M per month on Scale.** T2 costs Rp 4.7M with ops on Scale, but the v1 brief's floor is
  Rp 2.5M. Neon Launch cuts T2's infra to about Rp 1.8M (no SLA). Offer that at the low end, or raise the floor.
- **Neon compute is 60 to 90% of every paid tier's bill.** The levers are CU size, scale-to-zero windows and replicas.
  Tune them with benchmarks, not guesses.
- **T4 runs near Neon's ceiling**: 32 of the 56 CU fixed maximum. Prove it with a benchmark, or plan T4 on-premise.

## On-premise: when, what to buy, CapEx and OpEx

- **When.** Choose on-premise when:
  - Data must stay in Indonesia. Public-scope systems under PP 71/2019, and banks and financial firms under OJK
    rules, are the usual cases. Neon has no Indonesian region; the nearest is Singapore
    (`aws-ap-southeast-1`, neon.com/docs/introduction/regions, read 2026-10-21). Confirm the residency rules with
    counsel before a regulated bid.
  - The customer's policy requires its own hardware.
  - The load is past Neon's 56 CU ceiling.
- **Stack.**
  - The same Bun binary (`--role=web` behind HAProxy, `--role=worker` for projections and the outbox relay).
  - Postgres 17 with a synchronous standby (Patroni from T3).
  - pgBackRest to a backup node, plus an off-site copy in R2.
  - Prometheus, Grafana and Loki for monitoring.
- **What to buy.** The bill of materials is above. Hardware prices are budgetary: get at least two quotes from
  Indonesian distributors (Dell, HPE, Lenovo) before bidding.
- **Market reference.** Hetzner rents an AX162 (EPYC 9454P, 48 cores, 128 to 512 GB ECC, 2×1.92 TB NVMe) for €319 to
  €614 a month, excluding VAT (hetzner.com, read 2026-10-21). That is a sanity check on the DB node price, not an
  Indonesian option.
- **CapEx vs OpEx.** CapEx is spread over 60 months. Support after a 3-year warranty is 20% of CapEx over the life.
  OpEx is site (internet, or colocation with power), off-site backup and ops staff. Staff is the largest OpEx line at
  every tier.

## Evidence plan (ADR-0048)

1. **Generate volume.**
   - Post one month of tier volume through the command handlers (`NL_SCALE=t1..t4`, N9).
   - Bulk-copy older months as generated events.
   - Rebuild projections from event 0 and assert the trial balance matches.
2. **Drive load.** Run k6 `write-mix` (invoices, receipts, journals) and `report-mix` (Neraca, Laba/Rugi, aging,
   stock card) at the tier's peak: 10% of users active, 1 request per 3 s each.
3. **Size the database to the tier.** Use a throwaway Neon branch at the tier's CU, delete it afterwards, and record
   the run's cost. A 4-hour run costs about $17 at T3 and $92 at T4 (compute plus a day of storage at the prices
   above).
4. **Gate.** The SLO table above, plus a still-green Accurate zero-diff suite.
5. **Publish.** Write `docs/scale/results/<tier>-<yyyy-mm-dd>.md` with the commit, configuration, p50/p95/p99, error
   rate and cost. Sales material may cite only a tier whose latest result passes.

## When to move up a tier

Move up when any trigger holds for two weeks.

| From → to | Trigger |
|---|---|
| T0 → T1 | A signed customer, or real business data entered (risk R7) |
| T1 → T2 | More than 25 users, more than 5k documents a month, or report p95 above 3 s |
| T2 → T3 | More than 150 users, more than 50k documents a month, a database over 200 GB, or a residency requirement |
| T3 → T4 | More than 1,000 users, a second legal entity needing consolidation, or an RTO under 1 h |
