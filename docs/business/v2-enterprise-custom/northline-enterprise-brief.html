The pitch

A food group with three PTs, 1,200 staff and a central kitchen has two options. SAP quotes Rp 2B and 18 months. An Odoo partner will bend their recipes and batches into a generic module. Northline shows them their own ERP running on their own data in five working days. It quotes a fixed price after a paid blueprint and goes live in 12–20 weeks. Catalog modules are licensed as they are. New modules are co-built at up to 40% off, because Northline keeps the generic IP and resells it.

Who it's for

Indonesian groups with 200–3,000 staff, often several PTs, whose processes don't fit a package: food production, distribution, manufacturing, textile and project-based firms. Their budget is Rp 290M to Rp 1B and up.

The gap

Packages make you bend your processes to fit them. Software houses hand over code nobody maintains. Nobody sells a custom build that is also a maintained product.

Why now

Why now

AI made custom software cheap to build, but nobody has repriced it yet

Three things happened at once. The first is that the price of a custom build is still set by headcount. Indonesian software houses quote Rp 250M to Rp 2B+ for an enterprise system, taking 20–52 weeks with Rp 15–40M per developer per month. SAP-class projects start around Rp 2B and take 12–24 months. Those prices assume a team of eight to fifteen people. This repo shows one architect directing agents: 170 commits in two days and a ledger that matches a commercial product report for report. You can undercut every quote on the table and still earn a better margin than the vendors quoting.

Second, "AI-native ERP" became a headline without much behind it. Impact puts it on its homepage and HashMicro launched Hashy, but both bolt AI onto a packaged core. An event-sourced ledger is a better base for agents. Every change is an event with an actor, so an agent's proposal can go through the same approval workflow as a human's and be audited in the same log.

Third, enterprises have reasons to revisit their systems. Coretax reset tax integrations in 2025. The personal data protection law (UU PDP) has been fully in force since October 2024. And Q4 is when groups set next year's budgets. A conversation in November becomes a line item in the 2027 budget. A conversation in March waits for 2028.

Most "AI-native" claims stop at a chat box. Northline is AI-native in how it is built, how it runs and how it is priced. Only the first layer is proven today.

The verdict

Should you build this?

Reasons to build

  • One project pays more than v1's whole first year: Rp 290M–1B+ per signature, against Rp 245M from five SaaS customers.
  • AI changed your cost but not your buyer's price expectations. You can quote 30–50% under a software house and still keep a 60%+ margin.
  • Co-builds turn client money into product. Each new module is funded once and resold at catalog price, so services revenue builds IP instead of just headcount.
  • Your résumé fits what enterprise buyers check. You lead a multi-brand ERP replatform and serve as ISO 27001 management representative, and they want both.
  • You no longer fight Accurate on price. Enterprise buyers compare you with SAP and HashMicro quotes, where your price looks cheap.

Reasons to not build

  • Your employer is replatforming its own enterprise ERP with an outside vendor. A side business that sells enterprise ERP is the sharpest possible conflict. Without written consent, stop.
  • Enterprises buy from firms, not individuals. Vendor registration wants a PT, tax status, references and often company age. A three-month-old PT with no case study fails some procurement desks outright.
  • Delivery happens in the daytime. Blueprint workshops, UAT and go-live support run 9 to 5. A solo founder with a full-time job can't staff that, so you need a daytime functional consultant from the first deal.
  • Fixed-price custom work is how ERP vendors go broke. A loose blueprint or a scope creep clause you skipped eats the margin. Price risk is now yours, not the client's.
  • Services can trap you in project work. If co-built modules never get generalized and resold, you've built a software house, not a product company.

You have the credibility half: you've led an ERP replatform, run ISO 27001 and proven that agents can write most of the code. What you lack is the firm half. 0

Week 0 gate

Clear these before the first sales call.

  1. Employment contract and written consent. Pasal 36 makes the maker the default copyright owner, but employment contracts usually override it. Enterprise ERP is the category your employer is buying now, so get consent in writing or stop. Keep its vendors, its customers and the healthcare vertical out.
  2. Your own tools. Use your own Claude subscription and your own model API key, on personal accounts and hardware. Never use the employer's five Team seats.
  3. A firm enterprises can register. You need a PT with NIB and NPWP. Plan for PKP (VAT registration) early, because enterprise buyers need a tax invoice. A PT Perorangan works to start, but check with your first prospect's procurement team before relying on it. For a regular PT with your wife at 1%, ask a notary about joint marital property first.
  4. Contract templates reviewed by an IP lawyer. The MSA needs the co-build IP clause, a perpetual licence, source escrow, a 12-month exclusivity window, change-request pricing and a UU PDP data-processing addendum. Budget Rp 10–30M for the review.
  5. A daytime partner. Name the functional consultant or accountant who attends workshops and UAT. Pay them per project from the down payment.

What you'd sell

The value ladder.

The model follows Impact: a free look, a paid blueprint, a fixed-price build, then annual support. Two things are different. You own every line of code, so there is no Odoo licence underneath. And when the client needs something that doesn't exist yet, you build it at a discount in exchange for the right to resell it.

  1. 1
    Lead magnet
    Your ERP on your data, in five days (Rp 0)

    One critical workflow, built by agents on the prospect's own Excel exports, demoed on a throwaway instance (ADR-0032). This replaces the generic demo every competitor runs.

  2. 2
    Frontend
    Paid blueprint (Rp 35–75M, credited to the project)

    Three to four weeks of process mapping, gap analysis against the catalog, a migration dry run and a fixed-price proposal. The blueprint is the client's to keep even if they walk away.

  3. 3
    Core
    Configure · Extend · Co-build (Rp 290M–1B+)

    Configure (Rp 290–450M): catalog modules, migration, training, go-live. Extend (Rp 450–750M): adds client-specific workflows and integrations. Co-build (list Rp 1B+): new modules at 30–50% off when Northline keeps the generic IP. Paid in milestones of 30 / 40 / 30.

  4. 4
    Continuity
    Support, hosting and agents (15–20% of project value / year)

    A dedicated instance, backups and restore drills, upgrades, an SLA, AI agents with usage capped, and quarterly improvement sprints. On-premise is available as a premium tier.

  5. 5
    Flywheel
    Vertical packs, resold (at Configure price, ~75%+ margin)

    Each co-built module is generalized into a catalog pack: central kitchen, batch and expiry, textile costing. The next client in that industry pays Configure price for something that cost you nothing to build twice.

Full ownership

List price · e.g. Rp 1.08B

  • Generic module copyright assigned to the client
  • Full source code handed over
  • Northline cannot resell the module
  • Support optional, at 20% / year

Co-build (default)

30–50% off new modules · e.g. Rp 816M

  • Northline keeps the generic module's IP (Pasal 36 default, confirmed in the MSA)
  • Client gets a perpetual, royalty-free licence and source escrow
  • Client owns its data, configuration and client-specific extensions; none of it enters the product
  • Not resold to named direct competitors for 12 months
  • Resale credit: 5% of each resale goes to the client's support bill for 3 years
Code that clients pay to have built only becomes resellable if the codebase keeps the generic module separate from client-specific code. ADR-0033 proposes how.
