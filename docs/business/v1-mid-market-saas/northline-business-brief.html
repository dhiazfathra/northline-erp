The pitch

A distributor with three branches and 40 staff runs its books on Accurate. Approvals live in WhatsApp, consolidation lives in Excel, and Coretax broke their tax workflow in 2025. The next step up is Odoo or SAP Business One at Rp 230M to Rp 2B in year one. Northline imports their Accurate data, closes a month in parallel, and hands the accountant reports that match cell for cell. Then it adds what Accurate can't.

Who it's for

Indonesian companies with 20–300 staff and Rp 15B+ sales that have outgrown Accurate, Jurnal, Zahir or Excel but can't justify an SAP project. Trading and distribution first.

The gap

Everyone sells a new ERP. Nobody sells proof that your numbers come out the same after you switch.

Why now

Coretax reset every tax stack, AI collapsed build cost, and 1 January is ten weeks away.

Why now

Coretax reset the tax stack the same year AI made ERPs cheap to build

Three curves crossed. First, DJP's Coretax went live on 1 January 2025 and companies hit login failures, save errors and certificate problems. DJP kept the old e-Faktur running alongside it (KEP-54/PJ/2025), then tightened: PER-11/PJ/2025 requires e-Faktur through Coretax or an authorized PJAP, and PER-19/PJ/2025 lets DJP cut off invoicing access for repeat non-compliance from 22 October 2025. Invoices cancelled in e-Faktur Desktop showed up as still approved in Coretax. Every finance team re-examined its tooling this year.

Second, building an ERP stopped being the hard part. This repo has 170 commits from 18–19 October 2026, on top of two weeks of spec work, and reproduces 205 of Accurate's 229 Manufaktur reports with zero difference. A product that used to need a ten-person team for two years now fits in a solo founder's evenings. Distribution and trust are the scarce inputs.

Third, the calendar. Companies switch accounting systems at a period boundary, usually 1 January. Accountants and tax consultants have room in October to December and are buried from January until the corporate tax return deadline at the end of April. The partner-recruiting window is open right now.

The verdict

Should you build this?

Reasons to build

  • A 50–500× price cliff separates Accurate from Odoo and SAP, with no trusted product in between and 800K+ medium businesses sitting near it.
  • Zero diff is a trust asset nobody else sells. "Your reports will match" is checkable by the customer's own accountant, the person who vetoes ERP switches.
  • The product is unusually far along for a pre-revenue idea: 205/229 Manufaktur reports verified, retail and trade catalogs mostly done, an event-sourced ledger and 218+ tests.
  • You can pass a corporate vendor-security review: you lead a 13-person engineering org through an ERP replatform and serve as ISO 27001 management representative.
  • Expansion reuses the ledger. Payroll posts to the GL, HRIS sits on payroll, and vertical profiles (ADR-0031) are configuration on one core.

Reasons to not build

  • Employer conflict is binary. Your employer is mid-ERP replatform, clinics (its industry) are on your expansion list, and the five Claude seats are the employer's. Your own notes mark the contract check as unchecked.
  • It isn't pilot-ready: 9 of 11 web data modules are mock, the backend is single-company, and there is no Accurate importer, e-Faktur submission or restore drill yet.
  • System-of-record sales are slow. Corporates run vendor registration, want references and take 3–9 months, and a part-time solo founder is the bottleneck on every step.
  • Close emergencies land on days 1–5 of each month, during your office hours. You can't promise an SLA alone, so an accountant partner has to be first-line support.
  • Accurate can cut add-on prices tomorrow. The capture scripts in tools/accurate/ may also conflict with its terms: keep captures internal and market "migrates from Accurate", never "Accurate clone".

Founder fit

Not every good idea is good for you.

Who it's best for

✓Engineers who have shipped an ERP to production
✕Founders who can't sell to finance managers
✓Founders with an accounting-firm or tax-consultant network
✕Day job in the same category without written consent

You have the first half of this idea: the build, the security posture and the engineering credibility. You don't yet have the second half, a list of 20 finance managers or accountants who would take your call. That is the whole game for customer number one. An insider at an accounting firm gets a pilot in a week; a technical founder without that network spends months earning the same trust. So your first job is borrowing trust from people who already hold it (accountants, tax consultants, Accurate implementers), not writing more reports.

So, is it good for you?

Only if week 0 comes back clean. The build is done enough; your hours and your appetite for selling are the variables.

0

Week 0 gate

Clear these before writing more code.

  1. Employment contract. Read the IP, moonlighting and non-compete clauses. If ERP or accounting software is anywhere near your employer's business, get written consent or stop. Keep the clinic vertical and your employer's vendors out until you've left.
  2. Your own tools. Don't build on employer-provided Claude Team seats, laptops or hours: it strengthens an employer IP claim and may breach the Team plan's terms. Buy your own subscription and keep the repo on personal accounts.
  3. Legal entity. Corporate buyers need a PT with NIB and NPWP, and usually prefer a VAT-registered vendor. A PT Perorangan lets you start alone. For a regular PT with your wife at 1%, check with a notary first: many treat a husband and wife without a perjanjian kawin as one owner of joint marital property.
