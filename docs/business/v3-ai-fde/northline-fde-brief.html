The pitch

A distributor with 300 staff has 14 admins keying supplier invoices, a sales team that answers WhatsApp leads the next morning, and SaaS bills for tools most seats barely open. You measure it free in five days, then come back with a one-page offer in Rupiah. Six weeks later, invoices arrive as drafts for approval, leads get an answer in under a minute at 11pm, and SaaS renewals are cancelled because a tailor-made internal tool does the job with agents inside. The CFO sees the saving on next month's bills.

Who it's for

Indonesian companies with 100–2,000 staff and lots of executor work: distribution, manufacturing back offices, logistics, property, professional services, multi-outlet retail and F&B. The buyer is an owner, CEO, COO or CFO with a mandate to "do something with AI" and nobody to do it.

The gap

Consultancies stop at the deck, agencies at one chatbot, courses at the owner's laptop. Nobody embeds an engineer who ships agents on the company's own data and reports the Rupiah.

Why now

One engineer with agents is a team, and companies have the mandate but not the people

The mandate exists, and most companies are stuck at the basics. Raymond Chin, after hosting ~80 "AI one person company" founders, describes Indonesian owners and big firms that already have a mandate to implement AI but don't know how. In AWS's 2025 study, 28% of Indonesian businesses used AI, but 76% of those only for basic efficiency, and 57% named missing digital skills as the main barrier. The 2026 edition puts adoption at 40%. In Microsoft's 2024 survey, 92% of Indonesian knowledge workers already used generative AI while 48% of leaders worried their company had no plan.

Indonesian buyers pay for services more readily than for software, by Chin's account. An FDE sells the service and uses software as the delivery vehicle. And one engineer with agents is now a team: this repo shows 170 AI-authored commits in two days, which is the same speed that makes replacing a SaaS subscription cheaper than renewing it.

The FDE model is how the AI labs deploy. Palantir invented it ("one customer, many capabilities"). Anthropic hires FDEs at US$200–300K with 25–50% travel to customer sites, and OpenAI launched a deployment company in May 2026. a16z calls it trading margin for moat. On 3 June 2026 Anthropic wrote that "a successful pilot is not the same as a system a business can run on", and committed US$100M to services partners. None of them serve Indonesian mid-market companies.

SaaS replacement

The saving the CFO feels on the next invoice.

Seat-priced SaaS charges every user every month whether they open it or not. Zylo's 2025 index found US$4,830 of SaaS spend per employee and 52.7% of licences unused. A tailor-made tool, built by agents in days and run on the client's own instance, has no seat price.

Replace thin, seat-priced workflow tools: CRM pipelines, helpdesk, approvals and forms, project boards, simple trackers, dashboards. Leave alone payroll tax, accounting with Coretax (unless the Northline ERP covers it), email and office suites, security and identity, and anything with a regulator behind it. Score each tool first: bill ≥ Rp 100M a year (assumption), low feature use in the vendor's admin report, clean export, no regulatory engine, cancellable at the next renewal.

The verdict

Should you build this?

Reasons to build

  • Fastest first revenue of the three versions: a paid workshop or diagnostic in 30–60 days, against 75–105 in v2.
  • It sells what you already do at work: you lead an agentic-SDLC and AI transformation.
  • The saving is visible. A cancelled SaaS invoice and a ledger tied to payroll are easier to believe than an ERP business case.
  • Low capital. Martell's rule: solve it by hand first and get paid to learn.
  • It feeds v1 and v2. Embedded work surfaces ERP gaps, and every internal tool is a candidate catalog module under ADR-0033.

Reasons to not build

  • The overlap with your job is the skill itself. Consent must name what is yours and what is your employer's.
  • "Forward deployed" means daytime: shadowing, kickoffs and go-lives happen in working hours.
  • Every tool you build is a tool you maintain. SaaS replacement moves the vendor's maintenance to you, with a bus factor of one.
  • Services scale with people. Without a reusable library you've built a consultancy that stops when you stop.
  • Outcome pricing needs baselines. If the "before" isn't measured, every guarantee becomes an argument.

So, is it good for you?

Yes, as the first step, if consent is clean. It earns sooner than v2, needs less capital, and produces the references and reusable tools that make v2's enterprise projects easier to sell.

0

Week 0 gate

Clear these before the first advice call.

  1. Employment contract and written consent. Check the moonlighting, IP and non-compete clauses. The consent should name the line: no employer clients, vendors or competitors (healthcare, clinics and aesthetics stay out); no employer material (SOPs, prompts, ISO documents, code); outside working hours.
  2. Your own tools. Your own Claude subscription and model API key, on personal accounts and hardware. Never the employer's seats.
  3. An entity that can invoice. A PT Perorangan with NIB and NPWP is enough for owner-led companies. Ask larger buyers' procurement first, and plan PKP when a buyer needs a tax invoice.
  4. Contracts reviewed by a lawyer. An SOW with the guarantee and how it's measured; a UU PDP processing addendum (Art. 51; cross-border model calls signed off under Art. 56); the IP clause for generic agents and tools; SaaS-replacement subscription terms. Budget Rp 10–20M.
  5. A time model. Annual leave for kickoffs (statutory minimum 12 days a year), Saturday on-site sessions, executor screen recordings instead of live shadowing, and a daytime partner from the first sprint.
