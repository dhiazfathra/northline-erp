# Northline Forward: a self-employed forward deployed engineer who moves executor work to AI agents, and is paid from the hours, SaaS bills and revenue that change

> Founder brief v3, 2026-10-19. Companion page: [`northline-fde-brief.html`](northline-fde-brief.html). Source notes from the two videos: [`video-notes.md`](video-notes.md).
> Earlier versions: [v1 mid-market SaaS](../v1-mid-market-saas/northline-business-brief.md), [v2 enterprise custom ERP](../v2-enterprise-custom/northline-enterprise-brief.md).
> Figures are sourced or marked as assumptions. USD at an assumed Rp 18,000. Not legal or tax advice.

**How v3 differs from v1 and v2:** it doesn't sell an ERP. It sells you, embedded in a company as a forward deployed engineer (FDE), turning the manual work its executors do into AI agents and internal tools, so its managers can spend their time on strategy. Every engagement is priced and reported in Rupiah of three things: executor hours removed, SaaS subscriptions cancelled, and revenue added. The ERP from v1 and v2 becomes one of the tools you can deploy, not the thing you sell.

The idea comes from two videos (notes in [`video-notes.md`](video-notes.md)). [Raymond Chin](https://youtu.be/8Lz530r09WU) gives the four rungs of AI skill and the rule that Indonesian buyers pay for impact and services, not for AI or software. [Dan Martell](https://youtu.be/w-XPlC3a2oI) gives the sequence: find a painful problem, solve it by hand first, sell a one-page offer with a guarantee, then scale on agents instead of headcount.

## The pitch

A distributor with 300 staff has 14 admins keying supplier invoices, a sales team that answers WhatsApp leads the next morning, and SaaS bills for a CRM, a helpdesk and a project tool that most seats barely open. Northline Forward spends five days measuring it for free: hours per workflow, every subscription and its usage, and where leads leak. It comes back with a one-page offer in Rupiah. Six weeks later, invoices arrive as drafts for approval, leads get an answer in under a minute at 11pm, and two SaaS renewals are cancelled because a tailor-made internal tool does the same job with agents inside. The CFO sees the saving on the next month's bills, not in a slide.

## Who it's for

Indonesian companies with 100–2,000 staff and a lot of executor work: distribution and trade, manufacturing back offices, logistics, property and construction, professional services, multi-outlet retail and F&B. The buyer is an owner, CEO, COO or CFO who has a mandate to "do something with AI" and no one to do it.

- **The gap:** consultancies sell AI strategy decks; agencies sell one chatbot; courses teach owners to prompt. Nobody embeds an engineer who ships production agents on the company's own data and is measured on the Rupiah that move.
- **Why now:** AI made one engineer with agents as productive as a small team, and Indonesian companies have the mandate but not the people.

## What you sell

Northline Forward: an embedded engineer, fixed-price deployment sprints, a monthly retainer, and SaaS replacement at half the cancelled bill.

## Biggest risk

Your employer first: you lead its AI and engineering transformation, so a side business doing the same for other companies needs written consent and a clean line between its material and yours. Daytime presence second: "forward deployed" means sitting with executors during working hours.

## Why now: one engineer with agents is a team, and companies have the mandate but not the people

1. **The mandate exists, and most companies are stuck at the basics.** Chin, after hosting ~80 "AI one person company" founders, describes Indonesian owners and large firms that already have a mandate to implement AI but don't know how, in a market that is "sangat early" ([video, 00:30](https://youtu.be/8Lz530r09WU?t=30)). The data agrees. In AWS's 2025 study, 28% of Indonesian businesses used AI, but 76% of those used it only for basic efficiency, and 57% named a lack of digital skills as the main barrier ([AWS](https://press.aboutamazon.com/sg/aws/2025/8/new-aws-research-shows-strong-ai-adoption-momentum-in-indonesia)). The 2026 edition puts adoption at 40% ([Kompas](https://tekno.kompas.com/read/2026/08/07/13310077/adopsi-ai-di-indonesia-tembus-40-persen-aws-ungkap-banyak-perusahaan-belum-siap)). In Microsoft's 2024 survey, 92% of Indonesian knowledge workers already used generative AI, and 48% of leaders worried their company had no plan ([Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/id-id/2024/06/11/microsoft-and-linkedin-release-the-2024-work-trend-index-on-the-state-of-ai-at-work-in-indonesia/)).
2. **Indonesian buyers pay for services, not software.** Chin's claim: Indonesia has among the lowest willingness to pay for software and a relatively high willingness to pay for services ([video, 11:01](https://youtu.be/8Lz530r09WU?t=661)). An FDE sells the service and uses software as the delivery vehicle, which fits that buyer better than v1's subscription.
3. **One engineer with agents is now a team.** This repo is the receipt: 170 AI-authored commits on 18–19 October 2026, reproducing 205 of Accurate's 229 Manufaktur reports at zero diff. The same speed builds a client's internal tool in days, which is what makes replacing a SaaS subscription cheaper than renewing it.
4. **The FDE model is how the AI labs themselves deploy.** Palantir invented the role: "Dev: one capability, many customers. Delta: one customer, many capabilities" ([FDE Academy](https://fde.academy/blog/how-palantir-invented-the-forward-deployed-engineer-model), secondary). Anthropic hires FDEs at US$200–300K base with 25–50% travel to customer sites ([posting](https://jobs.accel.com/companies/anthropic/jobs/62104998-forward-deployed-engineer-applied-ai)). OpenAI launched a deployment company in May 2026 and agreed to buy Tomoro, which has ~150 FDEs ([The New Stack](https://thenewstack.io/forward-deployed-engineer-fde-openai-google/), secondary). a16z calls it "trading margin for moat": services-heavy early, then software margins ([a16z](https://a16z.com/services-led-growth/)). None of them serve Indonesian mid-market companies, so you would be the only FDE most of your buyers ever meet.
5. **Anthropic says the work is in integration.** On 3 June 2026 it wrote that "a successful pilot is not the same as a system a business can run on… The real work—and the real opportunity—is in the integration", and committed US$100M to services partners ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/services-track-partner-hub)). That is the closest real statement behind Chin's "humans helping humans adopt AI" line.

## The three value lines

Every engagement is reported monthly on a value ledger the CFO can check against payroll, bank statements and sales data.

| Line | What changes | How it's measured | Felt when |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Executor hours removed | Agents draft, check and route the work (invoice intake, reconciliation, reports, data entry, follow-ups); people approve the exceptions | Hours per workflow from screen recordings before; agent volume and exception rate after; × loaded hourly cost | Weeks 4–8 |
| 2. SaaS bills cancelled | A tailor-made, AI-native internal tool replaces a seat-priced subscription | The cancelled invoice | The next renewal date |
| 3. Revenue added | Leads answered in under a minute around the clock, faster quotes, dormant customers reactivated, overdue invoices chased | Against a 4-week baseline, and a holdout group where possible | Months 2–4 |

Removing executor work is sensitive in Indonesia. The default framing in every proposal is "absorb growth without hiring and move people to higher-value work", not layoffs. That is also the honest version of the numbers: hours removed are only cash when headcount growth stops or roles move, and the value ledger says which.

## SaaS replacement: the saving the CFO feels on the next invoice

This is the value line the client feels fastest. Seat-priced SaaS charges every user every month whether they open it or not. A tailor-made internal tool, built by agents in days and run on the client's own instance, has no seat price.

Zylo's 2025 index found an average SaaS spend of US$4,830 per employee, with 52.7% of licences unused ([Zylo](https://zylo.com/news/2025-saas-management-index), [mi-3](https://www.mi-3.com.au/17-01-2025/licence-spill-multimillion-dollars-saas-wasteland-cmos-can-not-afford-ignore)). Indonesian companies spend less per head, but they pay the same dollar seat prices for global tools:

**The counter-evidence is real.** Klarna dropped Salesforce and Workday, but mostly for other SaaS; its CEO later said "we didn't replace SaaS with an LLM" ([diginomica](https://diginomica.com/those-shutting-down-salesforce-and-workday-rumors-klarna-no-we-didnt-replace-saas-llm-admits-ceo)). Jason Lemkin replaced a paid tool with a vibe-coded app in a day and warns that "every app you build is an app you now have to maintain" ([SaaStr](https://www.saastr.com/the-90-10-rule-for-ai-agents-updated-we-replaced-a-paid-saas-tool-in-a-day-with-a-vibe-coded-app-heres-what-we-learned/)). One analysis puts the exposed tools at US$200–1,000 a month and single-purpose, not US$20–50 ones ([Elfin Growth](https://elfingrowth.substack.com/p/vibe-coding-wont-kill-saas)). That is why replacement is sold with a support line and only for tools that pass the score below.

### What to replace, and what to leave alone

Replace thin, seat-priced workflow tools: CRM pipelines, helpdesk and ticketing, approval and form tools, project boards, simple inventory or asset trackers, dashboards, survey and booking tools. Leave alone anything whose value is an engine you'd have to maintain: payroll tax (PPh 21, BPJS) and accounting with Coretax unless the Northline ERP covers it, email and office suites, security and identity tooling, and anything with a regulator behind it.

Score each subscription before offering to replace it:
- annual bill high enough to matter (assumption: ≥ Rp 100M a year)
- a low share of features in real use, from the vendor's admin usage report
- data that exports cleanly
- no regulatory engine inside
- a contract that can be cancelled at the next renewal without penalty

## AI-native, in three layers

| Layer | What it means | Status |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Built by agents | One engineer plus coding agents against acceptance tests. Internal tools in days, change requests in hours. This is why replacing a SaaS costs less than renewing it. | Proven in this repo |
| 2. Runs with agents | Agents do the executor work inside the tool: read documents, draft entries, answer customers, chase invoices. Each action is a proposal gated by approval rules (ADR-0028) and logged with its actor, so a human can see who (or what) did what. | Activity log with actor exists; report Q&A UI exists on mock data; no model backend yet |
| 3. Priced on outcomes | Fixed sprint prices sized at 25–40% of first-year value; SaaS replacement at half the cancelled bill; optional gain share on revenue. Model usage passed through at cost plus margin, with a cap. | Contract templates to draft |

Chin's enterprise point applies here: large companies worry about data leaking into AI. Each client gets its own instance (ADR-0032), and a per-client switch blocks cross-border model calls where UU PDP or the client's policy requires it.

## The verdict: should you build this?

### Reasons to build

- **Fastest first revenue of the three versions.** A paid workshop or diagnostic can land in 30–60 days, against 75–105 days in v2.
- **It sells what you already do at work.** You lead an agentic-SDLC and AI transformation; v3 packages that skill for other companies.
- **The saving is visible.** A cancelled SaaS invoice and a value ledger tied to payroll are easier to believe than an ERP business case.
- **Low capital.** No product has to exist before the first sale; Martell's rule is to solve it by hand first and get paid to learn.
- **It feeds v1 and v2.** Every embedded engagement surfaces the ERP gaps, and every internal tool is a candidate catalog module under the ADR-0033 boundary.

### Reasons not to build

- **The overlap with your job is the skill itself.** Your employer pays you to lead exactly this kind of transformation. Consent must name what is yours and what is theirs.
- **"Forward deployed" means daytime.** Shadowing executors, kickoff workshops and go-lives happen in working hours.
- **Every tool you build is a tool you maintain.** SaaS replacement moves the vendor's maintenance burden to you, with a bus factor of one.
- **Services scale with people.** Without a library of reusable agents and tools, you've built a consultancy that stops when you stop.
- **Outcome pricing needs baselines.** If the "before" isn't measured, every guarantee becomes an argument.

## Founder fit

| Best for | Not for |
|---|---|
| Engineers who have run an AI or automation transformation | Founders who can't sit with executors on a weekday |
| Founders who can talk Rupiah with an owner or CFO | Anyone whose employment contract claims all work in the same skill |

You have the delivery half: you've led an engineering org to ship mostly AI-authored code, you know ISO 27001, and you can build fast. The missing half is daytime presence and a business-side partner. Chin's advice is to share the work with someone whose skills complete yours ([video, 00:01](https://youtu.be/8Lz530r09WU)): an ops or finance person who runs discovery while you build.

**So, is it good for you?** Yes, as the first step, if consent is clean. It earns sooner than v2, needs less capital, and produces the references and reusable tools that make v2's enterprise projects easier to sell.

## Week 0 gate: clear these before the first advice call

1. **Employment contract and written consent.** Check the moonlighting, IP and non-compete clauses. The consent should name the line: no employer clients, vendors or competitors (healthcare, clinics and aesthetics stay out); no employer material (SOPs, prompts, ISO documents, code); outside working hours.
2. **Your own tools.** Your own Claude subscription and model API key, on personal accounts and hardware. Never the employer's seats.
3. **An entity that can invoice.** A PT Perorangan with NIB and NPWP is enough for owner-led companies. Ask larger buyers' procurement before relying on it, and plan PKP when a buyer needs a tax invoice.
4. **Contract templates reviewed by a lawyer.** An SOW with the guarantee and how it's measured, a UU PDP data-processing addendum (you are the processor under Art. 51; cross-border model calls need the client's sign-off under Art. 56), the IP clause for generic agents and tools, and the SaaS-replacement subscription terms. Budget Rp 10–20M.
5. **A time model.** Plan where daytime hours come from: annual leave for kickoffs (statutory minimum 12 days a year), Saturday on-site sessions, screen recordings from executors instead of live shadowing, and a daytime partner from the first sprint.

## The value ladder

The ladder follows Martell's order (advice, then a manual fix, then an offer, then the build) and prices on Chin's rule (the impact, not the AI).

1. **Lead magnet: hours and SaaS audit (Rp 0, five working days).** A 60-minute call, the client's list of SaaS invoices, and a day of screen recordings from five executors. Output: a one-page value map in Rupiah and a one-page offer.
2. **Frontend: workshop or diagnostic (Rp 15–50M, diagnostic credited to the sprint).** A half-day leadership workshop (rung 1), or a two-to-three-week diagnostic that baselines the workflows and scores every subscription.
3. **Core: deployment sprint (Rp 75–180M fixed, six weeks).** One workflow cluster taken to production with agents, connectors and an internal tool. Sized at 25–40% of first-year value. Paid 50 / 50.
4. **SaaS replacement (50% of the cancelled bill per month, or a buy-out).** Built in the sprint, run on the client's instance, saving felt at the next renewal.
5. **Continuity: FDE retainer (Rp 20–60M/month).** Embedded days each month, agent operations, model cost passed through with a cap, a new automation each month, and the monthly value ledger.
6. **Upside: gain share (10–20% of verified added gross profit, 12 months).** Only on revenue agents with a baseline or holdout group.
7. **Flywheel: the agent and tool library.** Generic agents, connectors and internal tools are kept under the ADR-0033 boundary and redeployed at the next client in days. Accounting and ERP replacements route to the Northline ERP (v1, v2).
